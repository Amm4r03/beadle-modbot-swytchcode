# What is a Google Developer Group (GDG)?

Sources: Google for Developers community pages (developers.google.com/community) and the GDG program. GDG chapters are volunteer-run local developer communities focused on Google technologies. Figures below are Google's published program claims.

Google Developer Groups are local communities of developers interested in Google products and APIs — Android, Google Cloud, Firebase, Flutter, machine learning and related tools. There are 2000+ community groups worldwide per Google's community page. Chapters are run by volunteer organizers, not Google employees, with support (branding, programs, expert speakers) from Google's community team.

A typical chapter runs free or low-cost meetups: evening talks, hands-on codelabs, study jams, and watch parties for events like Google I/O. Larger chapters host DevFest — the annual community-led tech conference season (Oct–Dec; 115+ countries, 500+ cities, 700+ events per the 2026 DevFest page). Events are listed on the chapter's community page; many also mirror announcements on meetup platforms, X, LinkedIn, and community chat groups.

What actually happens at a meetup [INFERENCE from program descriptions]: 1–3 short talks or a guided codelab, Q&A, networking over food, and follow-up links shared afterwards. First-timers can walk in — no membership, no fees, no vetting. The value for a newcomer is meeting working developers and seeing real tooling demos; for regulars it is the network and early word on programs like Build with AI.
