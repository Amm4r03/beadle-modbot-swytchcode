# Volunteering, speaking, and organizing with GDG

Sources: Google for Developers community hub ("Lead a GDG", "Lead a GDG on Campus", Google Developer Experts program) at developers.google.com/community.

GDG chapters run on volunteers. Ways to get involved, in increasing order of commitment: attend regularly and help newcomers; volunteer at events (registration desk, photography, hosting a codelab table); propose a talk — organizers welcome practitioner talks about real projects on Google stack, and first-time speakers are common at meetups [INFERENCE from program tone]; become a co-organizer by working with the existing team; or start a new chapter via "Lead a GDG" if your city or campus has none (there is a dedicated GDG on Campus track for students).

The Google Developer Experts (GDE) program is the recognition layer above organizing: practitioners who share deep expertise publicly can apply to become recognized community leaders. Separately, Google's Accelerators support startups building on Google tech — relevant if a side project outgrows the meetup stage.

Practical advice [INFERENCE]: the fastest path to a speaking slot is showing an organizer something you built — a demo beats a slide deck proposal. For organizers, the job is mostly logistics (venue, food, registrations, speakers) plus keeping the welcome warm for first-timers.
